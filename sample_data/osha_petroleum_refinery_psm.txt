## 1. Purpose & Background
This document highlights areas of the Process Safety Management standard (PSM - 29 CFR 1910.119) where OSHA issued the most citations during the Petroleum Refinery Process Safety Management National Emphasis Program (NEP).
Key high-citation areas include:
- Process Safety Information (PSI)
- Process Hazards Analysis (PHA)
- Operating Procedures
- Mechanical Integrity (MI)
- Management of Change (MOC)

Since the PSM standard was promulgated in 1992, the petroleum refining industry (SIC 2911, NAICS 32411) has had a high frequency of catastrophic incidents involving highly hazardous chemicals (HHC). In June 2007, OSHA initiated CPL 03-00-004 to audit refinery compliance and establish actionable remediation guidelines.

## 2. Process Safety Information (PSI) & RAGAGEP
Employers must compile written Process Safety Information (PSI) covering HHC hazards, process technology, and equipment design.

### Recognized and Generally Accepted Good Engineering Practices (RAGAGEP):
All equipment in PSM-covered units must comply with selected RAGAGEP (consensus codes, non-consensus documents, and internal engineering standards per 29 CFR 1910.119(d)(3)(iii)).

Key RAGAGEP Standards Cited:
- API 520: Sizing, Selection, and Installation of Pressure-Relieving Devices in Refineries. Defines relief system calculations based on flow rate, set pressure, and back pressure. Fluttering or chattering indicates excessive back pressure.
- API 521: Pressure-relieving and Depressuring Systems. Design basis for flare headers and depressurizing systems.
- ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code (BPVC), Section VIII, Division 1, UG-135(d) and Appendix M: Governs intervening stop valves between pressure vessels and relief devices to prevent accidental blockage of relief paths.
- API RP-752 / API RP-753: Management of Hazards Associated with Location of Process Plant Permanent and Portable Buildings (Facility Siting). Mandates maximum credible event modeling and blast-resistant control room separation.
- Process Industry Practices (PIP) PIC 001 & ISA ANSI/ISA-S5.1: Piping & Instrumentation Diagram (P&ID) documentation criteria and instrumentation symbology.

## 3. Process Hazards Analysis (PHA) — 29 CFR 1910.119(e)
A PHA is an organized, systematic review to identify and control hazards associated with HHC handling.
Must address:
1. Previous incidents with catastrophic potential.
2. Engineering and administrative controls.
3. Consequences of engineering/administrative control failure.
4. Facility siting (blast overpressure, toxic release distance).
5. Human factors (alarm overload, bypass valve accessibility, emergency egress).

### Recommendation Resolution (29 CFR 1910.119(e)(5)):
Employers must establish a formal tracking system to promptly address, resolve, and document all PHA team recommendations. Citations frequently involved unresolved recommendations older than 5 years (e.g., rerating overpressured coils, updating P&IDs, evaluating increased crude unit throughput effects on relief systems).

## 4. Operating Procedures & Safe Work Practices — 29 CFR 1910.119(f)
Written operating procedures must provide clear instructions for:
- Initial startup, normal operations, temporary operations, emergency operations, and emergency shutdown (29 CFR 1910.119(f)(1)(i)(D)).
- Safe Work Practices (29 CFR 1910.119(f)(4)):
  - Motorized equipment access control in ignition-controlled areas.
  - Line breaking and equipment opening.
  - Hot work permits (29 CFR 1910.252).
  - Lockout/Tagout (LOTO - 29 CFR 1910.147).
  - Confined space entry (29 CFR 1910.146).

## 5. Mechanical Integrity (MI) — 29 CFR 1910.119(j)
Covers pressure vessels, storage tanks, piping systems, pumps, relief devices, emergency shutdown systems, and instrumentation/sensors.

### Leading MI Deficiencies & Inspection Standards:
- API 510: Pressure Vessel Inspection Code (In-service inspection, repair, and alteration).
- API 570: Piping Inspection Code (In-service inspection, rating, and repair).
  - Class 1 Piping (flammable, H2S, anhydrous HCl, hydrofluoric acid): Thickness measurement inspection frequency must not exceed every 5 years.
  - Classes 2 & 3 Piping: Thickness inspection frequency must not exceed every 10 years.
  - Condition Monitoring Locations (CMLs): Must be established at injection points, mixing tees, and dead legs susceptible to localized corrosion.
- API RP-571: Damage Mechanisms Affecting Fixed Equipment in the Refining Industry. Addresses Corrosion Under Insulation (CUI).
- Resolving Anomalous Data: Inconsistent inspection readings (e.g., increasing pipe wall thickness) must be retested and formally evaluated, not discarded.

## 6. Management of Change (MOC) — 29 CFR 1910.119(l)
Any modification (except replacement-in-kind) to process chemicals, technology, equipment, procedures, or facilities requires a written MOC before implementation.
MOC requirements:
1. Technical basis for the change.
2. Impact on safety and health.
3. Modifications to operating procedures.
4. Authorization by designated management personnel.
5. Training of affected operators and maintenance staff before startup.
6. Defined time limits for temporary changes (e.g., temporary supports or temporary control rooms).
